Dark Souls rarely explains its story directly. Most of it is scattered across item descriptions, background details and short conversations. Here's a plain summary of the main story. **This post spoils the ending.**

## Before the fire

In the Age of Ancients, the world was gray and unformed, ruled by everlasting dragons who could not die. There was no life or death, no light or dark.

## The First Flame

Then came fire, and with it opposites: heat and cold, life and death, light and dark. From the dark, beings came to the flame and found powerful **Lord Souls** within it:

- **Gwyn**, who became the Lord of Sunlight
- **Nito**, the First of the Dead
- **The Witch of Izalith** and her daughters of chaos
- **The furtive pygmy**, a small, forgotten figure who found a different soul, the Dark Soul

With these powers, and help from the dragon Seath the Scaleless who betrayed his own kind, Gwyn and the other lords defeated the dragons. The **Age of Fire** began.

## The fading flame

Fire does not last forever. As the First Flame began to fade, the world started to decay, and humans began to be marked with the **Darksign**, the curse of the undead. Undead cannot truly die, but each death slowly wears away their mind until they go "hollow."

To keep his age alive, Gwyn traveled to the Kiln of the First Flame and gave himself to it, **linking the fire** and burning for a thousand years.

## Your role

You play an undead who answers an old prophecy. Two great serpents offer you competing paths:

- **Kingseeker Frampt** urges you to follow in Gwyn's footsteps and link the fire, extending the Age of Fire.
- **Darkstalker Kaathe** reveals that humanity descends from the pygmy's Dark Soul, and urges you to let the flame die and begin an **Age of Dark**, the age of humans.

## The two endings

At the end of the game you face Gwyn himself, now the burned-out Lord of Cinder. Afterward you choose:

- **Link the Fire**: you sacrifice yourself to keep the flame burning for another age.
- **The Dark Lord**: you walk away, and the fire goes out.

The game never says which is right. Is linking the fire a noble sacrifice or just delaying the inevitable? Is the Age of Dark freedom or ruin? That ambiguity is a big part of why fans are still arguing about it more than a decade later.
