Why Dark Souls Still Matters

opinionhistory

In this post

  2. Difficulty with a purpose
  3. Strangers who help
  4. Its legacy

When Dark Souls came out in 2011, it had no tutorial pop-ups, no quest markers and no map. It dropped you into a dying world and trusted you to figure it out. That trust is the heart of why it still matters.

A world that folds back on itself

The original Lordran is one of the best-designed worlds in games. You spend hours climbing away from Firelink Shrine, then open a door or ride an elevator and realize you are right back where you started. Every shortcut feels like a discovery, and the world starts to make sense in your head the same way a real place does.

Difficulty with a purpose

Dark Souls is hard, but it is rarely unfair. Enemies telegraph their attacks, your stamina bar makes every swing a decision, and almost every death teaches you something. The real reward is not beating a boss; it is noticing that the area that wrecked you an hour ago is now easy, because you got better.

Strangers who help

Players can leave short messages on the ground for each other. Most are helpful (“try jumping”, “hidden path ahead”), some are jokes, and a few are lies. Add summon signs that let another player join you for a tough fight, and a lonely game suddenly feels like a shared struggle.

Its legacy

FromSoftware, led by director Hidetaka Miyazaki, built on these ideas in Bloodborne, Sekiro and Elden Ring, and an entire genre (“soulslikes”) grew out of them. A remastered edition arrived in 2018, and it is still the best way to start.

If you have never played it, this blog is for you. Start with the beginner tips, and don’t go hollow.
